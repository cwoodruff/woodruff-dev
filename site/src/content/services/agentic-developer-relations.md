## Service Overview

Agentic Developer Relations is the discipline within Developer Relations responsible for ensuring that AI coding agents and autonomous AI systems can successfully integrate with, consume, and represent a platform accurately.

Your developer community has a new member. It does not attend your conferences or read your blog, but it touches every integration your platform hosts, and it makes a decision about your platform on behalf of every developer who asks it to write some code. Most DevRel programs have no visibility into what that member sees or how often it fails. I coined the term Agentic Developer Relations and wrote the framework for it. This service brings that framework into your organization.

The human track of Developer Relations does not go away. Conferences, office hours, community, and honest content become scarcer and more valuable as generated content floods the channels. The agent track runs alongside it, and the two share one goal: developers who choose your platform, stay, and tell other developers.

## Signals You Need This

- Developers report "it didn't work" integrations that never appear in your support queue, because the AI tool silently generated broken code and the developer moved on
- Buyers now evaluate your platform by asking an AI assistant to build a proof of concept instead of assigning an engineer for a week
- Your documentation is written for humans who navigate, not for agents that retrieve sections as context and amplify every gap into an integration failure
- Your SDK's error model and schemas are readable by people but not parseable by agents
- Tacit integration knowledge lives in a few DevRel heads and leaves with them
- Engineering wants evidence before investing in platform ergonomics, and "developer sentiment" is not evidence

## What's Included

- **FAISR baseline.** Of all integrations generated by AI coding tools using your published materials as context, what percentage produce working code without manual correction? I measure it across the major coding agents, per integration task, and against your closest competitor.
- **Agent-readiness audit** of documentation, SDKs, error models, schemas, and sample code, with a prioritized fix list engineering can act on.
- **Prompt-recipe library.** Validated, versioned recipes for your most common integration tasks, with coverage and freshness tracking so they do not rot when the API changes.
- **Measurement model** the board can read: FAISR, the Amdahl ceiling (how much of integration time is stuck at human speed), recipe coverage, recipe freshness, and competitive FAISR delta.
- **Agent Champion role design**: the job description, hiring rubric, and operating rhythm for the person who owns this after I leave.
- **Human-facing DevRel** when you need it: DX audits, content strategy, conference programs, community building, and DevRel team design. I led DevRel at JetBrains and Rocket and still ship architecture.

## How It Works

### Week 1: Diagnostic

Identify your ten most common integration tasks, write the prompts a typical developer would use, run them through at least two AI coding tools, and score the output. You get a FAISR baseline and a ranked list of where agents fail, in writing, in one week.

### Weeks 2 to 12: Program

Fix the highest-leverage documentation and SDK gaps, build the recipe library, instrument the measurements, and stand up the Agent Champion role. Human-track work (content, community, events) runs in parallel where it is in scope.

### Handoff

Your team owns the recipe library, the dashboards, and the operating rhythm. I step back to a light advisory cadence or out entirely.
